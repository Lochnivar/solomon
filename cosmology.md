# Cosmology

## The Hierarchy

**The Divine** — Off the board narratively. Present only in consequences and the original covenant. The source of Solomon's curse and the organization's foundational mandate.

**The Fallen** — Former divine beings. The original darkness. Ancient, strategically patient, operating through religious and political systems across millennia. Maybe a dozen significant ones still active. Structured as an anti-council mirroring Solomon's twelve tribes. Their ultimate goal is reintegration or domination on their own terms — a calculated reclamation of their original status. Many still carry the faded grandeur of their pre-Fall condition.

**Nephilim** — Offspring of the Fallen and human women. Killed in the Flood, but the condition persisted. Range from monster-adjacent to tragic figures. Some are the most reliable allies Solomon can find — their enemy list already overlaps with his.

**Demons** — Disembodied spirits of dead Nephilim. The splinter generation. Stripped of bodies, driven entirely by appetites they cannot satisfy directly. Hence the possession obsession. They have built their own chaotic networks outside the Fallen's direct control: lesser courts, possession chains, and the monster lineages they seed. Some remain exploitable by the Fallen; others have fully broken.

**Monster Lineages** — Nephilim-descended or demon-corrupted humans reproducing independently. Vampires, certain shapeshifter lines, others. Some victims, some predators, moral complexity throughout.

**Humans** — Including Solomon's organization. Entirely human, armed with three thousand years of accumulated knowledge.

## The Fallen and the Demons

The Fallen are the original darkness — ancient, disciplined, strategic. The demons are the splinter generation that broke from that tradition.

The core rift is ideological. The Fallen's power comes from patience: millennia of careful infiltration into political systems, religious institutions, and human power structures. They think in centuries. The demons, stripped of bodies and driven purely by appetite, have no capacity for that patience and no interest in it. From the demon perspective, the Fallen have become calcified — establishment figures who've organized and strategized so long they've forgotten what they actually are.

From the Fallen's perspective, the demons are exactly what happens when discipline is abandoned: loud, clumsy, appetite-driven chaos that draws attention to the supernatural underworld the Fallen have spent centuries keeping hidden. Every possession, every visible manifestation, every demon-caused incident that bleeds into human awareness is a threat to operations the Fallen have been running for a thousand years.

The relationship is parental in a disturbing way — the demons are literally the remnant souls of the Fallen's own children, killed in the Flood. After the Flood destroyed their bodies, the Fallen largely abandoned the demon population — treated them as collateral damage, or embarrassing mistakes. That abandonment is the wound that drives the breakaway as much as appetite does.

The three forces behind demon independence:
- **Power hunger** — they want bodies, sensation, and influence now. The Fallen's patience looks like cowardice or senescence from below.
- **Resentment** — many blame their progenitors for creating them, then failing to protect or empower them after the Flood.
- **Independence** — over millennia they built their own networks: lesser courts, possession chains, monster lineages seeded outside Fallen control.

The breakaway was gradual, accelerating during historical windows of chaos — the interwar period being the most documented, when Solomon's absence removed the primary check on demon activity and the Fallen were slow to compensate.

**Ashtoreth** occupies the most complicated position. She has the strongest maternal claim on the demon population — some demons still flock to her as a dark queen. Others regard her as the ultimate betrayer, the architect of their abandonment. Whether her continued investment in the demon population is genuine attachment, guilt, or a long strategic play is an open question even within the anti-council.

### Intra-Supernatural Conflict

The rift generates its own ongoing conflict:
- The Fallen sometimes reassert control — binding particularly dangerous or visible demons, or using demon populations as disposable tools when it suits them
- Demons occasionally overthrow or betray a Fallen when they sense weakness, especially during Solomon-induced power vacuums
- Some demons are potentially recruitable by Solomon's organization precisely because their hatred of the Fallen exceeds their hatred of his people — the enemy of an enemy problem

### Operational Implications

The ideological rift is exploitable. Dan's program of feeding Fallen intelligence about demon activity works partly because the tactical math supports it — but it works *reliably* because the Fallen's contempt for demon chaos is genuine. They will act on that intelligence not just when it serves them but because demon visibility genuinely threatens their long game. Triggering a Fallen purge of demon activity is a legitimate operational outcome.

The demons, for their part, don't organize against the Fallen the way they organize against Solomon. The Fallen are background, establishment, the world they emerged from. The relationship is more resentment than active conflict — though that could change during a sufficiently long absent period.

## The Triangular Battlefield

Not a binary good vs. evil axis. Three factions, each fighting the other two with shifting tactical alliances.

**Fallen vs. Demons** — Ideological contempt from above, resentful rejection from below. The Fallen suppress demon activity when it threatens their operations. Occasionally exploit demon chaos when it serves them. The relationship is dysfunctional family as much as it is enemy factions.

**Fallen vs. Solomon** — Personal, ancient, strategic, and by divine design. The Fallen's agenda and Solomon's mandate are not merely incompatible — the mandate was built to oppose them specifically.

The relationship originates in a deliberate Fallen operation: during the Temple construction, the Anti-Council impersonated the Archangel Michael to deliver the Seal and binding knowledge to Solomon during a moment of pride, fatigue, and desperation. The sophistication of the operation was theological before it was practical. The Fallen did not argue against God or Solomon's faith. They induced a feeling — that this crisis exceeded his prior provision, that the wisdom granted at Gibeon was not quite enough. An omniscient God's provision is complete: the Gibeon gift already accounted for Ornias, the Temple, and everything after. The prayer that said otherwise was the crack. The impersonation of Michael was just what walked through it.

God's response addressed the full situation. The demon mandate is the surface layer, but the curse was built wider. The Fallen ran the operation, seduced Solomon, and represent the original darkness behind everything the demons are. A complete response did not leave them out. In acquiring a proxy to manage their demon problem, the Fallen handed God the justification and the mechanism to create their permanent supernatural enemy. The operation that was meant to give them a useful tool produced instead a recurring, unkillable opponent whose explicit mandate includes them.

The Fallen set the terms of the curse's operational toolkit, and Solomon has been running on Fallen-sourced knowledge for three thousand years. What they did not anticipate: the permanence of the curse, the scope of the mandate they triggered, and what his organization would build from that original toolkit across thirty centuries.

**Solomon vs. Demons** — The original curse mandate. The day-to-day operational reality. Most of what the organization's combat and intelligence apparatus is built around.

### Tactical Alliance Patterns
- Dan feeds Fallen intelligence about demon activity when suppressing demons serves Solomon's interests
- Sometimes letting a Fallen operation run is correct because it is suppressing something worse
- The Fallen exploit demon incidents as cover for their own operations and as recruitment pressure on vulnerable humans
- Demons occasionally serve Fallen interests — wittingly or not — when their chaos creates the conditions a Fallen operation requires
