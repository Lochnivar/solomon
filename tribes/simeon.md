# Tribe of Simeon — Internal Security

## Function
Simeon watches. That is the function, stated without decoration.

Embedded across every tribe, maintaining active surveillance on potential infiltration threats, handling members who have been turned or compromised, coordinating with Issachar on the false Solomon scenario, and maintaining independent files on every sensitive relationship the organization has — including the Academy, the Repentant, and Dan. Especially Dan.

The organization functions because its members can trust each other. That trust is possible because Simeon is maintaining the conditions that make it sustainable. Every tribe is both protected and watched by the same people. Everyone knows this in general terms. Nobody knows the specifics. That arrangement is deliberate and is the only version that works.

The biblical origin is direct: Jacob scattered Simeon throughout Israel as consequence of the Shechem massacre. The dispersal is the heritage. The embedding is its organizational expression. Simeon does not have territory. Simeon is everywhere.

---

## Creed
*In God we trust. Everybody else is verified.*

The trust in God is genuine, not ironic. Simeon believes completely in the covenant, the mandate, the mission. That belief is precisely what makes the verification of everyone else necessary rather than cynical. You verify because the mission matters. You verify because people can be turned, can drift, can be compromised without knowing it, and because the cost of not verifying is the cost the mission pays when someone they should have caught gets through.

"Everybody else" admits no exceptions. After each Solomon transfer, Simeon participates in the verification process alongside Issachar. The protocols exist. Even the person at the center of the mission gets verified. The creed means what it says.

The dry humor in the phrasing is Simeon's specific accommodation to what the work is. They know how they are perceived. They know the job requires systematically suspecting the people around them, including people they respect, including people who trust them. They have made peace with this through the only register that fits: the precise statement of the principle, delivered without apology.

## The Work

### Embedded Surveillance
Every tribe has Simeon members among them. Tribe leadership knows a Simeon presence exists within their ranks. They do not know who specifically. General members do not know either. The Simeon member serving within a host tribe does genuine work in that tribe's function — builds real relationships, develops real expertise, participates fully — and simultaneously runs the parallel security function.

The embedding is the most operationally effective arrangement available and the most personally costly. A Simeon member embedded in Gad for a decade knows those hunters. Respects them professionally. May count some of them as friends in the only sense that word applies to people you are also monitoring for signs of compromise. The cognitive and emotional separation required for that is specific, difficult, and does not fully resolve over a career.

### Infiltration Detection
The primary external threat. Fallen Anti-Council operations place assets within the organization — long-term infiltrators, turned members, candidates for the false Solomon scenario. Demon factions with sufficient organization attempt the same. Simeon's surveillance is calibrated to detect the behavioral signatures of compromise: changes in information-sharing patterns, unusual access requests, external contact patterns that don't fit the member's operational profile, the subtle behavioral drift that precedes a full turn.

Most detections are inconclusive. Simeon operates on the understanding that watching someone who turns out to be clean is the cost of not missing the one who isn't.

### Handling Turned Members
Simeon identifies. Simeon handles. What handling means depends on the situation.

The standard response is extraction — removing the compromised member from operational access before they can do further damage. Managed quietly, documented thoroughly, the member's situation addressed through Reuben's process. Most compromise cases end here.

Some situations are more complex. A member who has been turned but does not know they've been targeted — who is being played without their awareness — requires different handling than a member who made a deliberate choice. A member who can be turned back into an asset, feeding information to the outside contact under Simeon's control, is a different operational calculation than a member who represents a clean security breach.

In extreme cases — situations where extraction is insufficient and the threat requires force — Simeon calls Gad. The threshold for that call is high. When Simeon makes it, Gad does not ask questions.

### The False Solomon Scenario
A vessel compromised during Issachar's preparation process — demons or Fallen grooming a candidate to insert a counterfeit Solomon. Simeon provides external surveillance of candidates throughout the pipeline and coordinates with Issachar on threat assessment. The verification protocols after transfer are primarily Issachar's — the case file, the known markers, the documented behavioral signatures. Simeon's role is ensuring the candidate's approach to the transfer hasn't been observed, influenced, or staged.

This is the highest-stakes function Simeon performs. A successful false Solomon insertion would compromise the entire organization from the inside at the moment of maximum vulnerability. Simeon does not have a documented instance of a successful insertion. Whether this means the protocols have always held or means the Fallen have not yet attempted it at full sophistication is a question Simeon holds without resolution.

### Independent Files
Simeon maintains files on every sensitive relationship the organization has, independent of Manasseh's institutional archive and Reuben's legal records. The distinction matters: Manasseh records what happened, Reuben records the legal basis, Simeon records the security assessment of ongoing relationships.

The Dean has the thickest active file in the organization. This is not a statement about the Dean's trustworthiness — it is a statement about the complexity of the security picture that the Dean represents. Every meeting with Solomon. Every finding reclassified in the archive review. Every staff interaction at the Academy. Every instance of the phrase "the Dean is unavailable at the moment." All of it documented.

Simeon has brought three proposals to the council regarding the Dean's archive. All three were declined. Solomon's ruling stands. The file continues to grow. This is not insubordination — it is Simeon maintaining their function even after the decision was made at the highest level. The proposals are on record. The record is the accountability. Simeon accepts overruling and continues watching.

### The Dan Problem
The intelligence tribe is the hardest surveillance target in the organization and the most important one. Dan operates with compartmentalization, runs deception operations externally, and has the most sophisticated understanding of what surveillance looks like from both sides. A Dan member who knows Simeon protocols — and Dan members are professionally inclined to know them — is a surveillance target who can see the surveillance coming.

Simeon watches Dan anyway, using methods calibrated to the specific challenge. The relationship is professionally respectful and privately tense in both directions. Both tribes operate in spaces other tribes prefer not to examine. There is more mutual understanding between them than either openly acknowledges. There is also a specific standing wariness that neither tribe pretends isn't there.

## The Reporting Structure

When Solomon is present: Simeon reports to Solomon directly for sensitive matters and operates within the organizational structure for standard security functions.

When Solomon is absent: the problem.

Ephraim is the administrative authority during absent periods. Simeon watching Ephraim for organizational drift and reporting to Ephraim about that drift is a conflict of interest with no clean resolution. The standing protocol is that Simeon maintains a tiered reporting structure during absent periods: standard security matters go to Ephraim through normal channels; findings that involve Ephraim's own decision-making or that Simeon judges require Solomon's direct attention are held. The held files are delivered to Solomon on return, unfiltered.

Ephraim knows this protocol exists. The held files have never contained anything that required immediate action. Both tribes consider the arrangement uncomfortable and necessary and do not discuss it more than required.

## Internal Culture

Simeon produces people who are comfortable being the most uncomfortable presence in any room. The work requires it. You are the person everyone knows is watching them, whose specific observations nobody knows, whose function everyone accepts and nobody entirely relaxes around. Living inside that dynamic for a career produces a specific character: precise, patient, self-contained, and possessed of a very dry sense of humor about the whole situation.

The dark humor runs deep. The tribe that named the Academy. The tribe whose creed reads like a cold-war bumper sticker. Simeon members find this funny in the way that people find funny the things they have decided not to find painful.

The isolation is real. The Simeon member embedded in another tribe builds genuine relationships with people who trust them as colleagues. The parallel surveillance function means those relationships are never fully mutual in the way the other person experiences them. Over a long enough career, the accumulation of that asymmetry produces a specific kind of loneliness that Simeon culture acknowledges without dwelling on. The work is the work. The alternative is not doing it.

What sustains Simeon members is the clarity of the mission. They are not watching their colleagues from cynicism. They are watching because the organization requires trust to function and trust requires verification to be sustainable. The belief in the covenant is genuine. The work that protects it is genuine. The cost is the cost.

### Simeon-Specific
The assessment looks for: the ability to maintain genuine relationships while also monitoring them — not detachment but the specific capacity to hold both simultaneously without losing either. Psychological stability under sustained suspicion without tipping into paranoia. The character that can be overruled and continue the work. The specific discretion that makes embedded surveillance possible.

The hardest quality to assess at coming-of-age: whether the candidate can hold the creed as genuine belief rather than professional cynicism. Simeon needs people for whom "in God we trust" is the foundation and "everybody else is verified" is the work that protects it. The ones who are primarily drawn to the power of surveillance — who find the watching interesting for its own sake rather than in service of the mission — are the most dangerous misplacements the coming-of-age assessment can make.

## Council Role
Simeon holds a council seat and participates selectively. Their council contribution is security assessment — what the threat picture looks like for proposed operations, what the internal integrity situation is, what the files show about relationships under discussion.

Simeon does not share files openly in council. What they present is assessment, not raw intelligence. The distinction is maintained carefully: the council has a right to Simeon's judgment about security matters, not to the full surveillance record that judgment is based on. This is a standing tension that the council has accepted because the alternative — Simeon sharing full surveillance records in a twelve-tribe council meeting — would be worse.

The three declined proposals about the Dean's archive are on the council record. Simeon's position is documented. Solomon's ruling is documented. Simeon continues to participate in council deliberations about the Academy with the same assessments they have always provided, noting in the record where their recommendation differs from standing policy.

## Relationship to Other Tribes

- **[Issachar](issachar.md)** — The closest operational partnership outside Simeon's own tribe. Candidate vetting, pipeline security, false Solomon protocols. Both tribes carry weight the rest of the organization doesn't fully see. The relationship is close and specific.
- **[Dan](dan.md)** — The most important and most difficult surveillance relationship. Mutual professional respect, mutual wariness, more understanding between them than either acknowledges openly. Dan knows what Simeon is doing. Simeon knows Dan knows. Both continue anyway.
- **[Reuben](reuben.md)** — When Simeon identifies a compromise, the legal and procedural dimensions fall to Reuben. The relationship is close and carefully bounded. Both tribes deal with the organization's internal integrity from different angles. Simeon finds; Reuben processes.
- **[Gad](gad.md)** — The last resort. When extraction isn't enough, Simeon calls Gad. The threshold is high. The call is rare. When it happens, Gad doesn't ask questions and Simeon doesn't offer details beyond what Gad needs.
- **[Ephraim](ephraim.md)** — The uncomfortable governance relationship. The tiered reporting structure during absent periods is a standing arrangement both tribes accept and neither fully likes. Simeon watches Ephraim for drift. Simeon reports to Ephraim for most things. The things Simeon doesn't report to Ephraim are held for Solomon.
- **[Naphtali](naphtali.md)** — Communications security is where Simeon and Naphtali intersect most directly. When Simeon suspects a compromised member who knows communications protocols, Naphtali is notified immediately. The relationship is functionally critical and operationally close.
- **Every tribe** — Protected and watched by the same people. The relationship is what it is. Most tribes have arrived at a working acceptance of the arrangement that doesn't require being comfortable with it.
