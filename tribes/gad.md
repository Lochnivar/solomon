# Tribe of Gad — The Hunters

## Function
The organization's hunting arm. When the supernatural world produces threats that require direct engagement — demons manifesting, Fallen operations that have metastasized beyond diplomatic or legal resolution, vampire networks, lycanthrope packs, cryptid entities operating outside any tolerable boundary — Gad is what the organization sends.

They are specialists, not soldiers. Three thousand years of operational history against every significant supernatural threat type has produced a tribe with deep, lineage-specific knowledge of their quarry. They know what they're hunting before they go in.

When Reuben's legal mechanisms and Zebulun's diplomatic reach have run their course and the problem is still standing, Gad is the answer. The last resort, and the credible threat that makes every earlier resort worth taking seriously.

Gad does not self-task. Another tribe identifies the need, exhausts its options, and requests Gad. The requesting tribe owns the decision. Gad executes it.

---

## Creed
*When it cannot be otherwise.*

Force is not the preference. It is the last honest answer to a problem that has exhausted every other one. The organization does not want to use Gad. When it cannot be otherwise, Gad is there — ready, trained, and without hesitation.

The secondary principle that defines the operational identity: **Gad never acts unilaterally.** This is not a constraint they resent. It is a point of professional pride. They do not start things. They finish them. The restraint is the professionalism.

## The Work

### The Escalation Position
Gad operates at the end of a deliberate sequence. Reuben's legal mechanisms and Zebulun's diplomatic reach are applied first — not as courtesy, but as doctrine. Force forecloses options. Every other tool is tried before the one that can't be undone. When those tools have genuinely run out, the request comes to Gad.

This positioning makes Gad the organizational deterrent. The reason adversaries negotiate, comply, and take Reuben's processes seriously is partly the knowledge that the organization has a last resort and will use it. Gad doesn't need to be deployed often for that to be true. They need to be credible. They are.

### Monster Lineage Operations
The primary and most varied domain of Gad's work. Vampires, lycanthropes, and a wider roster of cryptid lineages — each with distinct physiology, behavioral patterns, known vulnerabilities, and operational signatures. Gad maintains lineage-specific doctrine for every significant threat type, drawn from three thousand years of engagement records, Dan's current intelligence, and Levi's knowledge of supernatural weaknesses.

This is specialist work, not soldiering. A Gad team deploying against a vampire network knows that network's likely structure, the lineage's behavioral tendencies, the tools that work and the ones that don't, and what the engagement will look like before they arrive. The deep knowledge is the operational advantage.

Not every cryptid is a target. Some are neutral. Some have become allies or interested parties. Gad's doctrine includes rules of engagement for distinguishing threat from non-combatant, informed by Dan's target designation. A hunter culture that can't make that distinction eliminates allies — and Gad has been doing this long enough to have some.

### Demon Operations
Possession and manifestation events require containment before engagement. Gad coordinates closely with Levi for binding operations — the tools that work against demons are ritual in nature, and Gad's field effectiveness depends on having them and knowing how to apply them under pressure. Embedded Levi support for major operations; Gad members carry basic ritual training for smaller engagements. The ethical constraint around possessed hosts — you are often fighting a person — is built into doctrine, not left to individual judgment.

### Fallen Operations
Direct confrontation with a Fallen entity is almost never the right call. They are ancient, entrenched, and contingency-planned against physical force. Gad's Fallen doctrine centers on disruption: dismantle the operation, cut the supply lines, remove the human infrastructure, force them to rebuild what took decades to construct. You cannot kill a Fallen with a well-placed strike. You can make them start over.

### Internal Capability
Gad can be requested for internal enforcement. The mechanism exists, is formally recognized, and has never been invoked. Simeon handles internal security — surveillance, extraction, the quiet management of turned or compromised members. If Simeon ever identified something so severe that extraction wasn't the answer, the request would come to Gad. Every tribe knows this capability exists. Nobody has needed to test it.

## Internal Culture

Gad operates more like a professional contractor unit than a standing army. The work is dangerous, the quarry is varied, and the tribe's identity is built around specialist expertise rather than military bearing. Three thousand years of engagement records, lineage doctrine, and field experience produces hunters who are matter-of-fact about what they do.

The culture is practical, not heroic. Gad doesn't spend much time on the romance of the hunt. They study their quarry, maintain their skills, run their scenarios, and show up prepared. Dark humor is pervasive — an occupational response to the nature of the work that nobody examines too closely. It is not gallows bravado. It is just how people talk who regularly deal with things that would break most people.

Training covers both physical readiness and deep lineage knowledge. A Gad member who can't identify a vampire bloodline by behavioral signature isn't ready. The preparation is not purely tactical — it's scholarly in a specific, applied way. Keeping current on Dan's intelligence updates and Levi's ongoing supernatural knowledge is part of the regular work cycle, not supplementary.

The "never unilateral" principle is internally generated, not externally imposed. Gad hunters who have been doing this long enough know what happens when someone self-tasks — wrong target designations, missed intelligence, operational failures that could have been prevented by waiting for the full picture. The restraint is the professionalism.

The specific professional impatience is still present. Gad can see when a situation is past the point of negotiation. They wait. They do not interfere with the Reuben or Zebulun process. But experienced hunters watching a target situation drag on have a way of making that awareness visible to anyone paying attention.

Losses are handled analytically. After-action review is culturally mandatory — not as ceremony but as doctrine refinement. What went wrong, what the lineage intelligence missed, what the team did that failed and what succeeded. A member lost in the field represents an operational failure to be understood, not only a personal loss to be mourned. Both happen. The order matters.

## Membership

See [tribes-general.md](../tribes-general.md) for the organization-wide framework.

### Gad-Specific
The assessment looks for: physical capability, tactical intelligence, the disposition to maintain specialist readiness indefinitely, and — critically — discernment. Gad needs people who can distinguish a threat from a non-combatant when the target in front of them is a cryptid lineage that includes both. That judgment cannot be trained from scratch at coming-of-age; it has to be present in the temperament.

The "never unilateral" principle is evaluated as disposition, not compliance. The ones who chafe against it wash out or cause problems.

## Council Role
Gad holds a council seat and participates minimally in governance deliberation. Their council function is primarily presence — the other tribes know Gad is there, knows what's being discussed, and is available when the request comes. Gad does not advocate for positions or accumulate political relationships. They are not interested in the council's internal dynamics. They are interested in being ready when needed.

## Relationship to Other Tribes

- **[Dan](dan.md)** — Primary intelligence feed and source of target designation. Dan produces context, qualification, and — critically — the distinction between threat and non-combatant in the cryptid space. Gad trusts Dan's designation. The tension is structural: Gad wants operational clarity; Dan produces context and sometimes deliberate ambiguity. A standing friction both tribes consider normal.
- **[Levi](levi.md)** — Field dependency. Gad's effectiveness against supernatural threats requires Levi-provided tools and knowledge. The relationship is close and operational — Levi embeds with significant Gad operations; Gad trains on ritual application for smaller engagements. Mutual professional respect.
- **[Asher](asher.md)** — Gad's lineage-specific equipment comes through Asher's non-commercial supply chain. Silver, specialized compounds, tools effective against specific threat types. Asher sources what exists outside ordinary commerce; Gad specifies what the lineage doctrine requires.
- **[Benjamin](benjamin.md)** — Both martial tribes with clearly distinct roles. Benjamin protects principals. Gad engages threats. When Solomon is directly involved in an operation the roles converge and standing doctrine covers the overlap. No significant friction — the distinction is clean enough that competition doesn't arise.
- **[Reuben](reuben.md)** — Gad waits for Reuben to exhaust its options before being called. The relationship is respectful and occasionally impatient. Reuben knows Gad is watching the clock. Gad knows Reuben's process is legitimate. Both are right.
- **[Zebulun](zebulun.md)** — Same dynamic as Reuben, applied to diplomatic rather than legal tracks.
- **[Simeon](simeon.md)** — The internal security / force boundary. Simeon handles compromise and infiltration quietly. If Simeon ever needed Gad, the situation would already be beyond any quiet handling. That threshold has not been reached. Both tribes hope it never is.
