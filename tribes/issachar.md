# Tribe of Issachar — The Finders

## Function
Maintains the vessel pipeline — identifying, preparing, and holding candidate host bodies for Solomon's soul transfer. Maintains the three-thousand-year case file. Manages the succession gap between Solomon's death and his return to operational status.

## Internal Culture
- Functions more like a succession office running a live pipeline than mystics awaiting visions
- The work never stops — when Solomon is present and operational, Issachar is already preparing for his next death
- Probably has internal factions over methodology — specifically over vessel selection criteria and how many candidates to maintain simultaneously
- The length of every historical absence is an Issachar failure or an Issachar success. They carry that weight.

## The Case File
- Accumulated records of every past incarnation
- Includes: known markers, verification tests, documented failure modes
- Three thousand years of data — the most complete record of a single soul in human history
- How this knowledge is organized and transmitted internally: *to be developed*
- Tracks demon faction alignment across incarnations — which demon networks are "loyalist" (still within Fallen influence) vs. "breakaway" (fully independent). Relevant to operational threat assessment and to [Dan](dan.md)'s intelligence programs.
- The death cycle correlation is Issachar data as much as Manasseh data — they hold the incarnation timing records that map against historical catastrophe. The debate over causality (do his deaths enable catastrophe, or does catastrophe kill him) is partly an Issachar internal argument. The Bronze Age records are the most contested; the WWI/WWII cycle is the most thoroughly documented.

## The Pipeline
- Issachar maintains a bench of prepared candidate vessels at all times — identified, vetted, and held in readiness
- What makes a proper vessel: physical suitability, spiritual compatibility, practical context — and willing consent. The host must agree to the transfer fully and knowingly, understanding that their ego will be completely subsumed. Solomon asserts and the host ceases to exist as a self. Their memories remain, accessible to Solomon, but they do not survive as a presence. Issachar does not take unwilling hosts. This is doctrine, not preference — the organization is not in the business of body snatching.
- The consent requirement means Issachar is cultivating candidates toward a willing act of self-sacrifice, not simply identifying and holding suitable bodies. A candidate may be in relationship with Issachar for years before agreeing. Some never agree. The preparation process is as much pastoral as operational.
- Variable absence periods are partly explained by this: it is not only that a suitable vessel must be found, but that a willing one must be. Those are different problems. Finding someone physically and spiritually suitable who also chooses to give themselves to this is not reliably fast.
- Preparation process beyond consent: *to be developed — what changes between agreement and readiness*
- Pipeline depth varies — sometimes three candidates are ready; sometimes the bench is thin or empty
- An unexpected death with no prepared vessel means an absence measured in however long it takes to find and prepare one. Days if fortunate. Decades if not.
- Hiroshima is the clearest documented case of an unprepared pipeline meeting an unexpected death

## Vulnerability
- Most vital and most vulnerable part of the organization
- Primary demon and Fallen target — destroying Issachar's pipeline extends the absent period strategically
- Targeting prepared vessels directly is more efficient than targeting Solomon himself
- The false Solomon problem: a vessel could be compromised during preparation — demons grooming a candidate over years to insert a ringer

## The Martyrs
Within the organization, being identified as a candidate vessel is an honor — one of the highest the organization confers. Candidates who consent and complete the transfer are venerated as martyrs. Solomon insists on this without exception. Issachar holds the relationship with candidates from identification through preparation through the moment of transfer; they are the ones who know each martyr most completely before Solomon does.

The pastoral weight of this work is significant. Issachar cultivates candidates over years, sometimes decades, toward a willing decision of total self-sacrifice. They are present at the end. The emotional reality of that relationship — and what it costs — is part of Issachar's internal culture in ways the other tribes don't fully see.
