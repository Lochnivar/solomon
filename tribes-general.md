# Tribes — General Systems

Cross-tribal mechanics that apply to the organization as a whole. Individual tribe files note what is specific to them; the general framework lives here.

---

## Theology and Identity

The organization is Abrahamic in foundation — covenant theology, the God of Abraham, the law as operational reality. It is not Jewish, Christian, or Islamic, and does not identify as any of the three. Three thousand years of direct experience with supernatural reality has produced a theological position none of the mainline traditions have a category for. The organization understands those traditions, respects what each one got right, and is not defined by any of them.

Solomon's working summary: God is real, the covenant has operational implications, everything else is commentary.

The tribes vary in how much of any given tradition's culture they carry. Levi carries the most of the Levitical heritage. Others carry less. None treats any contemporary denomination as authoritative over what they have seen and done.

## Membership

### Family System
Tribal identity is primarily inherited. Members are born into tribes, raised within them, and shaped by their culture from childhood. The majority of members in any tribe were born into it. Tribal identity is cultural, not ethnic — family lines are not bloodline requirements so much as continuity of upbringing and culture.

### The Foster Network
The organization's work is dangerous. Members die in service. The foster network exists to absorb orphaned children and ensure they are raised within the organization rather than lost to the mundane world.

Foster placement follows a preference order:
- Within the child's birth tribe where possible
- Within the organization broadly if no suitable birth-tribe placement exists
- Cross-tribal placement is managed and tracked — tribal identity follows upbringing when birth-tribe placement isn't available

Tribes with higher casualty rates maintain more developed foster networks by necessity. The organizational-wide standards for foster placement fall under Reuben's oversight.

Leaving the organization is not a practical option. Members know too much and belong nowhere outside. The foster system exists partly because of this — children born into the organization have no other world to go to, and the organization takes that responsibility seriously.

### Coming of Age — The Assessment
At the equivalent of bar or bat mitzvah age, every child in the organization undergoes formal assessment and placement. This is the moment a member takes on their specific covenant role — the organizational equivalent of becoming responsible for one's own obligations under the covenant.

**How it works:**
- Birth tribe is the default placement — most children confirm the tribe they were raised in
- Transfers occur when temperament and aptitude point clearly elsewhere, or when organizational need requires it
- Tribal representatives may advocate for candidates they want placed with them
- The child's own inclination is considered but is not determinative

**The consent mechanic:**
The choice is technically the child's. By assessment age, after a childhood inside this world, surrounded by people who serve, with the mission as the water they swim in, the choice is almost always a foregone conclusion. The formal consent matters — theologically, within the organization's covenant framework, it matters — but the cultural reality is that no one raised in this world needs to be persuaded to serve.

The rare refusal is so uncommon that the organization maintains a formal process for it that almost never gets invoked — a supervised non-operational existence for people who know too much to release but genuinely cannot serve. An institutional category everyone knows exists and nobody ever wants to use.

**What each tribe looks for** is noted in the individual tribe files.

## The Martyr Tradition

Willing vessel candidates who complete the soul transfer to Solomon are venerated as martyrs within the organization. Solomon insists on this without exception. Their names and lives are held in Manasseh's archive as sacred record. The organization observes them. The world does not know and never will.

The martyrs gave something harder than death — their existence as themselves, willingly, for the mission. The internal veneration is the only acknowledgment they will ever receive. Solomon's insistence that it be real and sustained is non-negotiable.

## Council Structure

- All twelve tribes hold council seats
- Solomon holds absolute authority when present
- Ephraim presides during Solomon's absent years
- Governance during absent years is Ephraim's role — other tribes defer to this without contest
- Individual tribal relationships to the council are noted in tribe files
